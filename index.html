Austin Ferro, PhD

I am a research scientist at CSHL where I study the connectivity rules of the brain: the logic by which brain cells find, contact, and remodel one another. A century after Golgi and Cajal argued over how neurons connect, we can finally test what logic brain cells use to interact at incredible resolutions. I am interested in going beyond the unidirectional connectivity patterns of neurons by incorporating how glia integrate into neural circuits to assess how these multiunit-multidirectional connectivity patterns shape circuits in neurodevelopment, adult cognition, and in neurological disorders.

Current Work

In preparation

Glia–glia crosstalk: homeostatic control of OPC form and function by microglia

Ferro A†, Ademola A, Kahng J, Zhang C, Vita DJ, Chan G, Lin Q, Fallon T, Monteiro J, McNamara N, Miron V, Trottman J, Bergles D, Cheadle L†  († co-corresponding)

Oligodendrocyte precursor cells are the only non-neuronal cells in the central nervous system that receive direct synaptic input from neurons, yet what sets and maintains their synaptic and morphological state has remained unclear. Combining three-channel in vivo imaging in awake mice on a custom two-photon microscope I built, machine-learning analysis of serial transmission electron microscopy and super-resolution volumes, whole-cell slice electrophysiology, and single-cell transcriptomics, I find that microglia dynamically and homeostatically survey and control OPC process arbors in both mouse and human tissue.

Microglia converge on neuron–OPC and neuron–neuron synapses alike, form contacts enriched near neuron–OPC synapses and OPC phagolysosomes, and set both the density of OPC synapses and the rate at which OPCs engulf them. Together this identifies glia–glia crosstalk as a control point for the noncanonical neuron–OPC synapse.

Presented at NYU SPiNES · MGH · Hospital del Mar · SfN minisymposium

SNACC: open-source, model-independent quantification of cell–cell interactions and engulfment in three-dimensional tissue volumes

Ferro A†, Fallon T, Cheadle L  († corresponding)

Segmented Neighbor Analysis of Cellular Components is a Python framework for measuring cell-cell interactions in three-dimensional fluorescence volumes. SNACC provides a GUI interface to segmented objects from any model and reports interaction and engulfment metrics without requiring a bespoke classifier for each new experiment, using Cellpose models trained against OPCs, microglia, synaptic material, and lysosomal organelles and adapted for the highly ramified morphologies of glia.

As proof of principle, SNACC was used to test whether pharmacological inhibition of the TAM receptors Tyro3, Axl, and MerTK reduces glial phagocytosis of synapses in mouse cortex.

Builds on the engulfment protocol in Nature Protocols (2024)

Research

Discovering the connectivity rules of brain cells in health and disease

The ideological battle between Camillo Golgi's reticular theory and Santiago Ramón y Cajal's neuron doctrine was fought with relatively crude methods for assessing intercellular contact. We now have the technology to discover the logic underlying cellular connectivity directly, at resolutions with the potential to reshape our understanding of brain physiology as profoundly as the refutation of the reticular theory did. Pairing single-cell calcium imaging with cortical connectomics, for instance, has already begun to reveal rules that govern synaptic patterning and its relation to neural function.

Yet a century of this work has concentrated on unidirectional connectivity between neurons, and has largely ignored how non-neuronal glia integrate into circuits — even as glial modulation of circuits, both during development and in the adult brain, becomes increasingly well appreciated. That reductive view limits our understanding of how neural circuits truly form and function, and points to the need to elucidate the rules governing how glia interact with neurons, how they interact with one another, and how those interactions go awry in neurological disorders. I am building a research program around the holistic logic of how brain cells structurally and functionally interact, and how those connectivity patterns shape neurodevelopment and adult cognition in health and disease.

OPC–Synapse Interactions

Oligodendrocyte precursor cells engage in activity-dependent phagocytic engulfment of synapses during development and experience-dependent plasticity. I develop high-precision imaging and quantification tools to measure this process with single-synapse resolution.

Microglia & Neural Circuit Maturation

Microglia are dynamic sentinels of neural circuits. Using live two-photon imaging in awake mice, I observe how microglial surveillance and synaptic interactions are regulated by sensory experience in the developing visual cortex.

Glia in Neurodegeneration

My PhD work (Cvetanovic Lab, U Minnesota) established that Bergmann glia undergo biphasic gene expression changes in spinocerebellar ataxia type 1, shifting from neuroprotective to pathological states. These findings revealed glial-specific therapeutic targets in cerebellar ataxia.

Tool Development

I am a firm believer in open source toolkits that increase the accessibility and reach of science

  • C3P2

    In vivo time-lapse

    An unattended batch pipeline for three-channel two-photon recordings, asking whether microglia preferentially survey OPC-associated synapses and whether an experimental manipulation changes how often or how long they do so. C3P2 segments synapses, neurites, microglia, and OPCs with per-channel Cellpose models, corrects three-dimensional drift between timepoints, and tracks each synapse to a stable identity across the whole recording, so the same synapse can be followed through a manipulation.

    Contact is reported per epoch as probability, frequency, mean event duration, and total contact time, measured in parallel by proximity and by voxel overlap so that converging effects raise confidence. Segmentation is cached and per-recording failures are isolated, so one bad file never stops a batch. Outputs are CSVs, QC colocalization hyperstacks, Fiji ROI sets, and mutually registered 3D meshes.

    Used for the glia–glia crosstalk manuscript

  • SNACC

    Fixed tissue

    Segmented Neighbor Analysis of Cellular Components (SNACC) quantifies cell–cell interactions and engulfment in three-dimensional fluorescence volumes. It is model-independent: segmented objects from any model can be passed in, and SNACC returns neighbor, contact, and engulfment metrics per cell and per object. Cellpose models trained against OPCs, microglia, synaptic material, and lysosomal organelles ship alongside it, with output adapted for the highly ramified morphologies of glia.

    Manuscript in preparation · public release to follow

  • Contactomics

    Serial EM & connectomics

    A spatial-statistics pipeline asking whether cell-cell contact sites are non-randomly positioned relative to features on the cell surface, built against the MICrONS and H0 cubic-millimeter volumes of mouse visual cortex and human temporal cortex. For proof of principle, I use Contactomics to voxelize OPC and microglial meshes to find the shared voxels that constitute a contact, query axon-to-OPC synapses from CAVE, and test observed contact-to-synapse distances against Monte Carlo null models, with a parallel permutation test against skeleton branch points.

    Results aggregate across cells with paired and nonparametric tests, and cortical layer is calibrated per dataset from layer-specific pyramidal cell types rather than assumed. It grew out of the serial-EM analysis code behind my 2024 ultrastructural work.

    MIT licensed
